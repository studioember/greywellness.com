If you've struggled with insomnia — lying awake for hours, waking at 3am and unable to fall back asleep, dreading bedtime — you've probably tried things. Melatonin. Sleep hygiene tips. Maybe medication. And maybe none of it fully worked.

CBT-I is different. Here's what it is.

## The basics

CBT-I stands for Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia. It's a structured, short-term therapy — typically 6 to 8 sessions — that targets the thoughts and behaviors that perpetuate chronic insomnia.

It is **not** medication. It is **not** relaxation exercises or meditation (though those can be components). It is a clinical treatment with decades of research behind it, endorsed by the American College of Physicians as the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia.

## Why behavior matters in insomnia

Insomnia often starts with something identifiable: a stressful event, travel, illness. But it becomes chronic when the behaviors we adopt to *cope* with poor sleep accidentally reinforce it.

Staying in bed longer to try to get more rest. Napping to recover. Avoiding activities because you're tired. Watching the clock. These behaviors feel logical, but they fragment your sleep drive and train your brain to associate your bed with wakefulness.

## What CBT-I actually involves

**Sleep restriction** is often the most challenging component. It involves temporarily limiting time in bed to match your actual sleep — not as punishment, but to build the biological pressure that makes sleep deep and consolidated. It is uncomfortable at first. It works.

**Stimulus control** re-associates your bed with sleep rather than wakefulness, worry, or screens. The rule is simple: the bed is for sleep and sex only. If you're awake for more than 20 minutes, you get up.

**Cognitive restructuring** addresses the thoughts that keep you stuck — catastrophizing about the consequences of poor sleep, clock-watching, and the hypervigilance that makes your nervous system too alert to sleep.

**Sleep hygiene** is the part most people have already heard of. It matters, but it's rarely sufficient on its own.

## Who it's for

CBT-I is appropriate for adults with chronic insomnia — difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, occurring at least three nights per week for at least three months.

It's also effective for people with anxiety, depression, or chronic pain where insomnia is a significant component. In many cases, treating the sleep problem measurably improves the other conditions.
